High-Risk Pregnancy Care in Dhaka: What It Means and What to Expect

Being told your pregnancy is high-risk can feel alarming. This guide explains what the classification actually means, what causes it, and how specialist care in Dhaka helps ensure the best possible outcome for you and your baby.

Dr. Farzana Deeba August 2026 7 min read

What does "high-risk pregnancy" actually mean?

A high-risk pregnancy is one where the mother, the baby, or both have a higher-than-average chance of developing complications during pregnancy or delivery. It is a clinical classification — not a prediction of a bad outcome. The term simply means that closer monitoring and more frequent specialist input are required.

In Dhaka and across Bangladesh, high-risk pregnancies are increasingly common due to rising rates of conditions like diabetes, hypertension, and advanced maternal age. With appropriate specialist care, the vast majority of high-risk pregnancies result in healthy mothers and healthy babies.

What makes a pregnancy high-risk?

There are three broad categories of risk factors:

Maternal health conditions

  • Gestational diabetes — develops during pregnancy and requires careful blood sugar management
  • Chronic hypertension or pre-eclampsia — high blood pressure that can affect the placenta and fetal growth
  • Thyroid disorders — both hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism can affect fetal development
  • Heart or kidney disease
  • Autoimmune conditions such as lupus
  • Obesity — associated with higher rates of gestational diabetes, hypertension, and caesarean delivery

Pregnancy-related factors

  • Multiple pregnancy — twins or triplets carry higher risks for both mother and babies
  • Placenta previa or placental abruption
  • Preterm labour history — previous early delivery increases risk of recurrence
  • Recurrent miscarriage — two or more pregnancy losses
  • Conceived through IVF or IUI — slightly higher risk of multiple pregnancy and preterm birth

Age-related factors

  • Advanced maternal age (35+) — higher risk of chromosomal abnormalities, gestational diabetes, and hypertension
  • Teen pregnancy — higher risk of preterm birth and pre-eclampsia

Important: Having one or more risk factors does not mean your pregnancy will have complications. It means your care team will monitor you more closely — which is precisely how complications are caught early and managed effectively.

How is high-risk pregnancy managed in Dhaka?

More frequent antenatal visits

Where a standard low-risk pregnancy typically involves monthly check-ups in the first and second trimesters, a high-risk pregnancy may require fortnightly or even weekly visits depending on the condition.

Additional ultrasound scans

Growth scans, Doppler blood flow studies, and cervical length measurements may be recommended at regular intervals to monitor the baby's development and the health of the placenta.

Specialist referrals

Depending on the condition, your obstetrician may coordinate care with other specialists — an endocrinologist for diabetes management, a cardiologist for heart conditions, or a neonatologist to prepare for early delivery if required.

Planned delivery

Many high-risk pregnancies are delivered before their due date — either by planned induction or elective caesarean section — to avoid the risks associated with going past a safe gestational age. This decision is always made collaboratively with the patient.

Warning signs to watch for during pregnancy

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

  • Severe headache or visual disturbances
  • Sudden swelling of the face, hands, or feet
  • Reduced or absent fetal movements
  • Vaginal bleeding at any stage of pregnancy
  • Severe abdominal pain or cramping
  • Fever above 38°C
  • Difficulty breathing

High-risk pregnancy care in Dhaka — what to look for in a specialist

When choosing a specialist for a high-risk pregnancy in Dhaka, look for a gynaecologist or maternal-fetal medicine specialist with specific experience in your condition. Key questions to ask:

  • How many high-risk pregnancies do you manage each year?
  • Which hospital will I deliver at, and what neonatal facilities are available?
  • How will my condition be monitored throughout the pregnancy?
  • When would you recommend delivering, and what method?
