PCOS Treatment in Bangladesh: Symptoms, Causes and What to Do Next

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome affects 1 in 10 women in Bangladesh. If you have been diagnosed with PCOS — or suspect you might have it — here is what you need to know about symptoms, treatment, and your fertility options.

Dr. Farzana Deeba June 2026 7 min read

What is PCOS?

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is one of the most common hormonal disorders affecting women of reproductive age in Bangladesh and worldwide. It occurs when the ovaries produce excess androgens (male hormones), which disrupts the normal menstrual cycle and can lead to the development of small fluid-filled sacs (follicles) on the ovaries.

Despite its name, not all women with PCOS have cysts on their ovaries — and not all women with ovarian cysts have PCOS. The diagnosis is based on a combination of symptoms, blood tests, and ultrasound findings.

What are the symptoms of PCOS?

PCOS presents differently in different women. Common symptoms include:

  • Irregular or absent periods — cycles that are longer than 35 days, or fewer than 8 periods per year
  • Difficulty conceiving — PCOS is one of the leading causes of infertility in Bangladeshi women
  • Excess hair growth (hirsutism) — on the face, chest, or back
  • Acne and oily skin
  • Hair thinning or hair loss
  • Weight gain — particularly around the abdomen
  • Darkening of the skin — in skin folds such as the neck, armpits, or groin

Note: Many women with PCOS have no obvious symptoms and only discover they have the condition when they struggle to conceive. If you have been trying to get pregnant for over 12 months without success, a hormonal evaluation is strongly advised.

How is PCOS diagnosed in Dhaka?

A PCOS diagnosis in Bangladesh typically involves:

  • Blood tests — measuring LH, FSH, testosterone, insulin, and thyroid hormones
  • Pelvic ultrasound — to assess the ovaries and uterine lining
  • Medical history review — including menstrual patterns, weight changes, and family history

At least two of three criteria are needed for a confirmed PCOS diagnosis: irregular ovulation, elevated androgen levels, and polycystic ovaries on ultrasound.

How is PCOS treated in Bangladesh?

There is no single cure for PCOS, but the symptoms can be effectively managed. Treatment in Dhaka depends on your specific concerns — whether that is regulating periods, managing symptoms, or achieving pregnancy.

Lifestyle changes

For women who are overweight, even a 5–10% reduction in body weight can significantly improve hormonal balance, restore regular periods, and improve fertility. A balanced diet low in refined carbohydrates and regular physical activity are the first-line recommendations.

Medications for PCOS

  • Metformin — improves insulin sensitivity and can restore ovulation in some women
  • Oral contraceptive pills — help regulate periods and reduce androgen symptoms (not suitable if trying to conceive)
  • Clomifene or Letrozole — ovulation induction medications used when trying to get pregnant
  • Anti-androgen medications — for managing hair growth and acne

Fertility treatment for women with PCOS

PCOS is highly treatable when it comes to fertility. Most women with PCOS can conceive with appropriate treatment:

  • Ovulation induction with Clomifene or Letrozole is often the first step
  • IUI (Intrauterine Insemination) — combined with ovulation induction for improved results
  • IVF — recommended when other options have not succeeded

Can women with PCOS have a healthy pregnancy?

Yes — the majority of women with PCOS who receive appropriate treatment go on to have healthy pregnancies. However, PCOS does carry a slightly higher risk of gestational diabetes, high blood pressure, and preterm birth, so specialist monitoring during pregnancy is important.

When should I see a specialist?

You should consult a gynaecologist or infertility specialist in Dhaka if:

  • Your periods are consistently irregular or absent
  • You have been trying to conceive for 12 months (or 6 months if over 35) without success
  • You have significant hair growth, acne, or weight gain that is affecting your quality of life
  • A previous doctor has mentioned PCOS but you have not received a clear treatment plan
