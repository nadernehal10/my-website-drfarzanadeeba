How to Prepare for Your First Gynaecology Appointment in Dhaka

Visiting a gynaecologist for the first time can feel daunting. This practical guide tells you exactly what to expect, how to prepare, and what questions to ask — so your first appointment in Dhaka is as useful as possible.

Dr. Farzana Deeba September 2026 5 min read

Why seeing a gynaecologist matters

Many women in Bangladesh delay seeing a gynaecologist due to unfamiliarity with what the appointment involves, cultural hesitation, or simply not knowing when to go. Regular gynaecological care is important at all stages of life — not only when there is a problem, but for preventive health checks, contraception advice, and fertility planning.

When should you see a gynaecologist?

  • You have been trying to conceive for 12 months without success (or 6 months if over 35)
  • Your periods are painful, irregular, very heavy, or have stopped
  • You have pelvic pain or pain during intercourse
  • You have unusual vaginal discharge or bleeding between periods
  • You need contraception advice or a cervical smear
  • You have been diagnosed with PCOS, fibroids, endometriosis, or a cyst
  • You are pregnant or planning a pregnancy and want specialist input
  • You are approaching menopause and experiencing symptoms

How to prepare for your appointment

Information to have ready

  • Your menstrual history — the date of your last period, how regular your cycles are, how many days they last, and how heavy the bleeding is
  • Any symptoms — when they started, how often they occur, what makes them better or worse
  • Previous medical history — past surgeries, medical conditions, previous pregnancies
  • Current medications — including any vitamins or supplements
  • Family history — particularly of gynaecological cancers, PCOS, or endometriosis

Documents to bring

  • Any previous ultrasound reports, blood test results, or referral letters
  • Results of any previous semen analysis (if attending for infertility)
  • Your partner's details if relevant (blood group, any relevant test results)

Practical tip: Write down your questions before the appointment. It is easy to forget things once you are in the consultation room. No question is too small or embarrassing — your gynaecologist has heard everything and is there to help.

What happens during a first gynaecology consultation?

History taking

Your doctor will begin by asking about your symptoms, medical history, menstrual cycle, previous pregnancies, and any medications. This typically takes 10–15 minutes and is done before any examination.

Physical examination (if indicated)

Depending on your symptoms and reason for attending, the doctor may perform a pelvic examination. You will always be asked for your consent first, and you can decline or ask for the examination to be deferred. A chaperone (female assistant) is available on request.

Investigations

Blood tests and an ultrasound scan are commonly arranged at or after the first appointment. You may be asked to return for a follow-up once results are available.

Discussion and plan

Your doctor will explain their initial findings and recommend next steps — whether that is further testing, medication, lifestyle changes, or a referral.

Questions worth asking at your first appointment

  • What tests do you recommend and why?
  • How long will it take to get results?
  • What are the possible causes of my symptoms?
  • When should I come back, and what should I watch for in the meantime?
  • Are there any lifestyle changes that could help my condition?
  • What are my treatment options if something is found?
