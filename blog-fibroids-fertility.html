Fibroids and Fertility — What Every Bangladeshi Woman Should Know

Uterine fibroids are found in up to 70% of women by the time they reach 50. While many cause no problems, some types can affect fertility and pregnancy. This guide explains everything you need to know about fibroids and how they are treated in Dhaka.

Dr. Farzana Deeba September 2026 7 min read

What are uterine fibroids?

Uterine fibroids (also called leiomyomas or myomas) are benign (non-cancerous) growths of muscle and fibrous tissue that develop in or around the uterus. They are extremely common — studies suggest that up to 70% of women will develop fibroids by the age of 50, though many never experience any symptoms or complications.

Fibroids are not cancerous, and they very rarely transform into cancer. However, depending on their size and location, they can cause significant symptoms and affect fertility.

Types of uterine fibroids

Fibroids are classified by their location within or around the uterus:

  • Submucosal fibroids — grow into the uterine cavity. These have the greatest impact on fertility and menstrual bleeding, even when small.
  • Intramural fibroids — grow within the muscular wall of the uterus. The most common type.
  • Subserosal fibroids — grow outward from the outer surface of the uterus. Often cause pressure symptoms but less likely to affect fertility unless very large.
  • Pedunculated fibroids — attached to the uterus by a stalk, either inside or outside.

Symptoms of uterine fibroids

Many women with fibroids have no symptoms at all. When symptoms do occur, they may include:

  • Heavy or prolonged periods — the most common symptom. Can lead to anaemia.
  • Pelvic pain or pressure — a sensation of heaviness or fullness in the lower abdomen
  • Frequent urination — if fibroids press on the bladder
  • Constipation — if fibroids press on the bowel
  • Pain during intercourse
  • Difficulty conceiving
  • Pregnancy complications — including miscarriage, preterm labour, or malpresentation

Note: The size of a fibroid does not always predict symptoms. A small submucosal fibroid inside the uterine cavity can cause significant bleeding and fertility problems, while a large subserosal fibroid may cause no symptoms at all.

How do fibroids affect fertility?

Not all fibroids affect fertility. The impact depends primarily on location:

  • Submucosal fibroids — clearly reduce fertility and implantation rates, and increase miscarriage risk. Surgical removal significantly improves outcomes.
  • Large intramural fibroids (over 4–5 cm) — may distort the uterine cavity or block the fallopian tubes, reducing fertility.
  • Subserosal fibroids — generally do not affect fertility unless very large.

Diagnosis of uterine fibroids in Dhaka

Fibroids are commonly diagnosed by:

  • Pelvic ultrasound — the standard first-line investigation, widely available in Dhaka
  • Saline infusion sonography (SIS) — fluid is instilled into the uterus during ultrasound to better visualise submucosal fibroids
  • MRI — used when multiple fibroids are present or when more detailed mapping is needed before surgery
  • Hysteroscopy — direct visualisation of the uterine cavity

Treatment options for fibroids in Dhaka

When no treatment is needed

Small, asymptomatic fibroids that are not affecting fertility or quality of life can be monitored with regular ultrasound. Many fibroids shrink naturally after menopause.

Medical treatment

  • Hormonal medications to manage heavy bleeding
  • GnRH analogues to temporarily shrink fibroids before surgery
  • Tranexamic acid or anti-inflammatory drugs for symptom control

Surgical treatment

Hysteroscopic myomectomy
Removal of submucosal fibroids through the cervix using a hysteroscope. No incisions required. Significantly improves fertility.
Laparoscopic myomectomy
Keyhole removal of intramural or subserosal fibroids. Preserves the uterus and fertility. Preferred for women wishing to conceive.
Open myomectomy
For very large or multiple fibroids not suitable for laparoscopy. Longer recovery but preserves the uterus.
Hysterectomy
Removal of the uterus. Definitive treatment only for women who have completed their family and have severe symptoms.
