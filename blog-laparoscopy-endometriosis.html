Laparoscopic Surgery for Endometriosis — A Complete Patient Guide

Endometriosis affects millions of women worldwide and is one of the leading causes of pelvic pain and infertility in Bangladesh. Laparoscopic surgery offers both diagnosis and treatment — this guide explains what to expect before, during, and after the procedure.

Dr. Farzana Deeba August 2026 8 min read

What is endometriosis?

Endometriosis is a condition where tissue similar to the lining of the uterus (endometrium) grows outside the uterus — on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, bladder, bowel, or pelvic lining. Like the uterine lining, this tissue responds to hormonal changes each month, causing inflammation, scarring, and adhesions (bands of fibrous tissue that bind organs together).

In Bangladesh, endometriosis is significantly underdiagnosed — many women suffer for years before receiving a correct diagnosis. It is estimated that 1 in 10 women of reproductive age has endometriosis, and it is found in 20–50% of women with infertility.

Symptoms of endometriosis

  • Painful periods (dysmenorrhoea) — severe cramping that disrupts daily life, often beginning before the period starts
  • Pelvic pain — chronic or cyclical pain in the lower abdomen or back
  • Painful intercourse (dyspareunia)
  • Painful bowel movements or urination — especially during menstruation
  • Heavy or irregular periods
  • Difficulty conceiving — endometriosis is a leading cause of infertility
  • Fatigue — particularly around menstruation

Important: The severity of symptoms does not always correspond to the extent of disease. Some women with minimal endometriosis have severe pain; others with extensive disease have few symptoms. This is why investigation is essential — symptoms alone cannot confirm or rule out the diagnosis.

The four stages of endometriosis

I
Minimal
Small implants, no significant adhesions. Fertility may be preserved.
II
Mild
Shallow implants on ovaries and pelvic lining. Some scar tissue.
III
Moderate
Deep implants, endometriomas (ovarian cysts), significant adhesions.
IV
Severe
Extensive disease with large endometriomas, dense adhesions affecting multiple organs.

Why laparoscopy is the gold standard for endometriosis

Laparoscopy (keyhole surgery) is currently the only definitive way to diagnose endometriosis. Ultrasound and MRI can detect endometriomas (ovarian cysts caused by endometriosis) but cannot reliably detect smaller implants or adhesions. A laparoscopy allows the surgeon to directly visualise the pelvis, confirm the diagnosis, and — crucially — treat the disease in the same procedure.

What happens during laparoscopic surgery for endometriosis?

Before the procedure

You will be admitted on the day of surgery and placed under general anaesthesia. The procedure typically takes 30 minutes to 2 hours, depending on the extent of the disease.

During the procedure

The surgeon makes 2–4 small incisions (5–12mm) in the abdomen. Carbon dioxide gas inflates the abdominal cavity to create space. A laparoscope (thin camera) is inserted, and the surgeon examines the pelvis, ovaries, tubes, and surrounding structures. Endometriotic tissue is then removed or destroyed using one of two techniques:

  • Excision — cutting out the endometriotic tissue completely. This is the preferred technique as it removes the root of the lesion and has lower recurrence rates.
  • Ablation — burning or destroying the tissue using heat or laser. Simpler but associated with higher recurrence.

Adhesions (scar tissue) are also carefully divided to restore normal pelvic anatomy. If endometriomas are present, they are drained and their inner lining is removed.

After the procedure

Most patients go home the same day or after one overnight stay. Common post-operative symptoms include mild shoulder pain (from the gas), abdominal soreness, and fatigue for a few days.

Recovery from laparoscopic endometriosis surgery

  • Day 1–3: Rest at home. Mild pain managed with simple analgesics.
  • Day 3–7: Gradual return to light activities. Avoid heavy lifting.
  • Week 2–4: Most women return to work (depending on the nature of their job).
  • 6 weeks: Full recovery, including resumption of exercise and intercourse.

How does laparoscopy help with fertility?

Laparoscopic surgery for endometriosis can significantly improve fertility outcomes by:

  • Removing endometriomas that may compress healthy ovarian tissue
  • Dividing adhesions that distort the tubes or ovaries
  • Restoring normal pelvic anatomy to allow natural conception or improve IUI/IVF outcomes
  • Reducing inflammation in the pelvic environment that can impair egg quality and implantation

Studies show that surgical treatment of Stage I–II endometriosis significantly improves natural conception rates. For Stage III–IV, IVF following surgery typically gives the best outcomes.

When should you consider laparoscopy?

Discuss laparoscopy with your gynaecologist if:

  • You have severe period pain that does not respond to painkillers or hormonal treatment
  • Ultrasound has detected an endometrioma (chocolate cyst) on the ovary
  • You have been trying to conceive for 12 months without success and endometriosis is suspected
  • You have deep pain during intercourse
  • You have unexplained infertility and other causes have been excluded
