What is IUI? A Complete Guide to Intrauterine Insemination

If you have been told that IUI might help you conceive, this guide explains exactly what the procedure involves, who it is suitable for, and what success rates look like in Bangladesh.

Dr. Farzana Deeba June 2026 6 min read

What is IUI?

IUI — or Intrauterine Insemination — is a fertility treatment where prepared sperm is placed directly into a woman's uterus around the time of ovulation. The goal is to increase the number of sperm that reach the fallopian tubes, improving the chances of fertilisation.

It is one of the most commonly performed and least invasive fertility treatments available in Dhaka and across Bangladesh. Unlike IVF, IUI does not involve egg retrieval or laboratory fertilisation — the sperm and egg meet naturally inside the body.

Who is IUI suitable for?

IUI is typically recommended for couples facing one or more of the following:

  • Unexplained infertility — when no clear cause has been found
  • Mild male factor infertility — low sperm count or motility that is not severe
  • Cervical factor infertility — when the cervical mucus may be preventing sperm from reaching the egg
  • Ovulation disorders — such as irregular cycles or PCOS, often used alongside ovulation induction medication
  • Single women or same-sex couples using donor sperm

Important: IUI is not suitable for women with blocked fallopian tubes, severe endometriosis, or severe male infertility. A thorough evaluation is essential before deciding on any fertility treatment.

How does the IUI procedure work?

Step 1 — Ovarian stimulation (optional)

In many cases, the woman is given oral medication (such as Clomifene) or injections to stimulate the ovaries to produce one or more eggs. Ultrasound monitoring tracks follicle development to determine the ideal time for the procedure.

Step 2 — Sperm preparation

On the day of the procedure, the male partner provides a sperm sample. The sample is processed in a laboratory to concentrate the healthiest, most motile sperm — a process called sperm washing.

Step 3 — Insemination

The prepared sperm is loaded into a thin, flexible catheter and gently placed into the uterus through the cervix. The procedure takes only a few minutes and is generally painless, similar to a cervical smear. Most women can resume normal activities the same day.

Step 4 — Waiting and testing

A pregnancy test is recommended approximately 14–15 days after the procedure. We advise against testing earlier, as this can give inaccurate results.

What are the success rates for IUI?

Success rates for IUI vary depending on age, diagnosis, and whether ovarian stimulation is used. Generally:

  • Women under 35: 15–20% per cycle
  • Women aged 35–40: 10–15% per cycle
  • Women over 40: 5–10% per cycle

Most specialists recommend attempting IUI for 3–6 cycles before considering other options such as IVF.

What should I do next?

If you have been trying to conceive for 12 months (or 6 months if you are over 35) without success, it is time to seek specialist advice. A comprehensive fertility evaluation — including hormonal tests, semen analysis, and ultrasound — will help determine whether IUI is the right first step for you.
