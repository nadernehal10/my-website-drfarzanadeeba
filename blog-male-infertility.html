Male Infertility in Bangladesh — What Couples Need to Know

Male factor infertility is responsible for approximately 40% of all infertility cases — yet it is often overlooked or left uninvestigated. This guide explains the causes, how semen analysis works, and what treatment options are available in Dhaka.

Dr. Farzana Deeba September 2026 6 min read

Why male infertility is often missed

In Bangladesh — as in many parts of South Asia — infertility investigations often focus disproportionately on the female partner. This leads to delayed diagnosis, unnecessary procedures for women, and missed opportunities for treatment. The reality is that male factor infertility contributes to approximately 40% of cases, and in a further 20%, both male and female factors are present.

A semen analysis is one of the simplest, least invasive fertility tests available — and it should be one of the first investigations done when a couple is struggling to conceive.

What causes male infertility?

Sperm production problems

  • Azoospermia — no sperm in the ejaculate. Can be obstructive (blockage) or non-obstructive (production failure).
  • Oligospermia — low sperm count (below 15 million per ml)
  • Asthenospermia — poor sperm motility (movement)
  • Teratospermia — abnormal sperm morphology (shape)

Structural and medical causes

  • Varicocele — enlarged veins in the scrotum that raise testicular temperature and impair sperm production. The most common treatable cause of male infertility.
  • Previous infections — sexually transmitted infections, mumps orchitis
  • Hormonal disorders — low testosterone, elevated FSH
  • Genetic conditions — Klinefelter syndrome, Y chromosome microdeletions
  • Obstruction — blockage in the vas deferens or epididymis
  • Previous surgery — hernia repair, vasectomy

Lifestyle factors

  • Smoking — significantly reduces sperm count and motility
  • Excessive heat — prolonged use of laptops on the lap, hot baths, tight clothing
  • Obesity
  • Stress
  • Certain medications — anabolic steroids, some antibiotics, chemotherapy

Important for Bangladeshi couples: A semen analysis does not require intercourse, does not carry stigma in a medical context, and can be done at most diagnostic centres in Dhaka within 24–48 hours. It is the essential first step for any couple investigating infertility.

Understanding semen analysis results

A semen analysis measures several parameters. Normal values (WHO 2021 reference ranges) are:

Sperm count
16 million/ml or more (total count 39 million or more per ejaculate).
Motility
42% or more total motility; 30% or more progressive motility.
Morphology
4% or more normal forms (Kruger strict criteria).
Volume
1.4 ml or more per ejaculate.

A single abnormal result should always be repeated after 2–3 months, as sperm quality can vary significantly. The entire sperm production cycle takes approximately 72 days.

Treatment options for male infertility in Dhaka

  • Lifestyle modifications — stopping smoking, weight loss, avoiding heat — can improve sperm parameters significantly within 3 months
  • Varicocele repair — surgical ligation of varicoceles can improve sperm count and motility in suitable candidates
  • Hormonal treatment — for hypogonadism and hormonal disorders
  • IUI — suitable for mild to moderate sperm abnormalities
  • IVF with ICSI — for severe male factor infertility, where a single sperm is injected directly into each egg
  • Surgical sperm retrieval — for azoospermia, sperm can be retrieved directly from the testis (TESA/TESE) and used for ICSI
