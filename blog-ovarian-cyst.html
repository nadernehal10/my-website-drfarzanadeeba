Ovarian Cyst Treatment in Dhaka — When Is Surgery Needed?

Ovarian cysts are extremely common and most resolve on their own. But some types require treatment — and knowing the difference is essential. This guide explains the types of ovarian cysts, when to seek care, and when surgery is recommended in Dhaka.

Dr. Farzana Deeba September 2026 6 min read

What is an ovarian cyst?

An ovarian cyst is a fluid-filled sac that forms on or within an ovary. They are very common — most women will develop at least one in their lifetime, and the majority are benign (non-cancerous) and cause no symptoms. Many resolve on their own without any treatment.

However, some cysts grow large, cause symptoms, affect fertility, or carry a risk of malignancy. This is why any cyst found on ultrasound should be evaluated by a gynaecologist.

Types of ovarian cysts

Functional cysts (most common)

These develop as part of the normal menstrual cycle and usually disappear within 1–3 months without treatment:

  • Follicular cysts — form when a follicle fails to release an egg and continues to grow
  • Corpus luteum cysts — form after ovulation when the empty follicle fills with fluid

Pathological cysts (require attention)

  • Endometriomas (chocolate cysts) — caused by endometriosis. Blood-filled cysts that can damage ovarian tissue and significantly affect fertility.
  • Dermoid cysts (teratomas) — contain tissues such as hair, skin, or teeth. Usually benign but require surgical removal.
  • Cystadenomas — develop from the outer surface of the ovary and can grow very large.
  • Polycystic ovaries — multiple small follicles associated with PCOS, not true cysts.

Note about cancer risk: The vast majority of ovarian cysts in women of reproductive age are benign. Malignant ovarian cysts are more common after menopause. Your specialist will assess risk based on ultrasound features, blood tests (CA-125), and your age.

Symptoms of ovarian cysts

Many cysts cause no symptoms and are found incidentally on ultrasound. When symptoms do occur, they may include:

  • Pelvic pain or pressure — dull ache on one side
  • Bloating or fullness in the abdomen
  • Pain during intercourse
  • Irregular or painful periods
  • Frequent urination (if the cyst presses on the bladder)

Emergency symptoms — seek immediate care

  • Sudden, severe pelvic pain — may indicate cyst rupture or ovarian torsion (twisting)
  • Pain with fever and vomiting
  • Signs of shock — dizziness, rapid breathing, weakness

When is surgery needed for an ovarian cyst in Dhaka?

Not all ovarian cysts require surgery. Your gynaecologist will recommend surgery if:

  • The cyst is large (generally over 5–6 cm) and not shrinking on monitoring
  • It is an endometrioma causing pain or affecting fertility
  • The ultrasound features suggest it may not be benign
  • The cyst has caused ovarian torsion (a surgical emergency)
  • The cyst has ruptured and is causing significant bleeding
  • It is a dermoid cyst (these do not resolve and must be removed)

Laparoscopic (keyhole) surgery for ovarian cysts

The preferred surgical approach for most ovarian cysts is laparoscopy — minimally invasive keyhole surgery performed through 2–3 small incisions. Benefits include:

Minimal scarring
Small incisions (5–10mm) rather than a large open cut.
Faster recovery
Most women return home the same day or after one night.
Ovary preservation
The cyst wall is carefully removed while preserving as much healthy ovarian tissue as possible.
Less pain
Significantly less post-operative pain compared to open surgery.
